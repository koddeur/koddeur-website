# Vibecoded Websites: How to Spot Them and Why You Should Avoid Them

Over the past few months, I've opened dozens of sites from startups, freelancers and personal projects. And I kept feeling like I was seeing the same site over and over.

Same purple gradient, same little icons, same phrases like "Boost your productivity." Nothing memorable.

This is what we call a **vibecoded** site. In this article, I explain how to recognize one, why it hurts you, and what I concretely do to avoid it on the sites I deliver.

## What Is a Vibecoded Site?

A vibecoded site is one generated almost entirely by AI, through repeated prompts. It can be reworked, but very often you start by vaguely describing what you want, accept whatever comes out, and publish.

The problem isn't AI. I use it every day to code. The problem is that an AI without precise instructions always takes the most probable path. And the most probable path is the average of all the sites it has seen.

The result: thousands of people who ask for "a modern site for my business" get almost the same site.

## The 6 Signs That Give Away a Vibecoded Site

If you tick more than three boxes, your site probably looks generated.

1. **The purple-blue gradient.** In the hero background, on buttons, in the text. It has become the default visual signature of generated sites.
2. **Emojis everywhere.** A 🚀 in front of every title, a ✨ in front of every benefit. It fills space, but it says nothing.
3. **Generic copy.** "Boost your productivity," "The all-in-one solution," "Join thousands of users." Sentences you could paste onto any product.
4. **The same structure.** Centered hero, three feature cards with icons, testimonials, three-column pricing, FAQ. Always in that order.
5. **The same components.** The same rounded cards with soft shadows, the same font, the same buttons. You recognize the library before you recognize the brand.
6. **No real images.** Generic illustrations or generated visuals, never a photo of the product, the team or the founder.

## Why It's a Real Problem

A site that looks like all the others is a site people forget. And on the web, you have a few seconds to convince someone.

**You lose credibility.** Visitors have seen these codes hundreds of times. Subconsciously, they think: "this was done in ten minutes." And if the site was made that fast, why would the product or service be any different?

**You lose trust.** Vague text doesn't answer the real questions: who you are, what exactly you do, for whom, and why you. Without those answers, nobody gets in touch.

**You don't stand out.** If your site resembles your competitor's, the client will choose on price. A strong identity is what keeps you out of that game.

## How I Avoid This on My Projects

I use AI to go faster, not to decide for me. Here's my method.

1. **I set an art direction before writing a single line of code.** Two or three colors, one font, an image style. For my Koddeur account, for example: black, white, one bold font and a single yellow accent. It's simple, but it's recognizable.
2. **I write the copy myself, or with the client.** Concrete sentences, with numbers, names and examples. "We deliver your site in 3 weeks" speaks louder than "A fast and efficient solution."
3. **I remove the superfluous.** No decorative emojis, no gradient without a reason, no section just because "all sites have one."
4. **I use real images.** Photos of the product, the team, behind the scenes. Even imperfect, they inspire more confidence than a generic illustration.
5. **I break the default structure.** I start from what the visitor needs to understand first, not from a template. Sometimes that makes a very short page, sometimes a very visual one.
6. **I give the AI precise instructions.** I provide the art direction, the copy and the components I want. It executes, I review and adjust every detail.

## In Summary

AI isn't the problem. The problem is letting it choose for you. A site that feels like you requires clear direction, sincere copy and a bit of care in the details.
