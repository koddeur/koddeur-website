## Why is it important to sort your inbox?

<mark>Today</mark> everything goes through email (bills, health insurance, the various **insurance policies** for your vehicles, deals, etc.) except that after a while you end up receiving several dozen a day, and you drown under the sheer number of emails, and the problem is you miss the ones that are really important and that you actually care about.

That's why it's important to sort it out once and for all — it takes a few hours, but once it's done it lets you feel relieved, clear your head, and be ready to handle the next email as it comes in. Because yes, once your inbox is empty you almost look forward to the next one just so you can file it away or decide its fate.

## Sorting

When you start sorting, you need one clear idea in your head: every email must leave the inbox — either it's filed away, archived, or deleted, but in every case it has to be dealt with, even if that takes a few minutes.

The first goal is to delete every newsletter — if you haven't already read it, it's already too late to read it now. Start with the ones spamming you the most: find the newsletter's email address easily in one of its emails, copy it, and paste it into the search bar — you can then select everything and delete it all at once. Repeat this process for every sender that spams you, and you'll be able to clear out half of your entire inbox. From experience, Vinted, Amazon, LinkedIn, and Leboncoin are among the apps that spam the most.

*Important: before deleting a newsletter, you need to unsubscribe from it — legally, every email must have a link somewhere in the message allowing the user to unsubscribe (often tucked away in tiny text at the bottom). It takes 30 seconds to unsubscribe and it's the most important step, so don't hesitate to do it: if the email hasn't been opened, it means the newsletter isn't interesting and probably won't be in the future either (95% of them won't be).*

After doing this first pass, keep going the same way with the other apps (Facebook, Twitter, Insta, etc.)

This will let you do a massive cleanup — the "important" emails that need handling will stand out much more easily and make you want to deal with them.

## Keeping it up

To keep your inbox clean, you need to make sure you're able to handle every email you receive. So you need to reduce the volume, which is why it's important to unsubscribe from 95% of newsletters, even 99%, and you need to stay well organized with the right categories (nothing stops you from creating a new category if you find it relevant).

I might sound obvious here, but to maintain a clean inbox you actually have to read your emails. Every email received should be handled within 48 hours max, 24 hours if possible. You should treat emails like text messages: as soon as you see one, you deal with it. Even if it's just to send it straight to the trash, you still have to do it. By doing this, you'll even be happy to receive emails, because you know it will be information that actually interests you.
